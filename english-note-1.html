A Letter to God

First Flight · English

🔖

1. About the Story

"A Letter to God" is written by G.L. Fuentes, a Mexican writer. It tells the story of Lencho, a farmer who has complete, unwavering faith in God — so much so that when a hailstorm destroys his entire corn crop, he writes a letter directly to God asking for one hundred pesos to sow his fields again and support his family until the next harvest.

Summary

Lencho's house sits on the crest of a low hill, and from there he can see his ripe cornfield below, dotted with the flowers that always promise a good harvest. All he needs is rain, or at least a heavy dew, and his family is confident of a good crop that year. But instead, a huge hailstorm arrives, destroying every single plant in his field within minutes — leaving the family with nothing to eat that year.

Key line to remember: Lencho tells his family, "A plague of locusts would have left more than this... The hail has left nothing: we will have no corn or beans this year." This shows how completely the storm destroyed their livelihood.

That night, Lencho's only worry is not despair but hope — hope in God. He writes a letter addressed simply "To God," asking for a hundred pesos so he can resow his land and survive until the next crop, and posts it himself. The postmaster, moved and amused by the letter's faith, decides to collect money from himself and his employees to send Lencho some help, though they could only gather 70 pesos, less than what he asked for.

The Twist

When Lencho returns to the post office and finds the money, he isn't surprised that God replied — only upset that the amount is short. Sitting down to write another letter, he asks God to send the rest of the money, but adds a sharp postscript: not to send it through the post office, "because the employees are a bunch of crooks," revealing the story's gentle irony — his faith in God remains absolute, even as his trust in people is shaken.

QUICK RECAP

  • Lencho — a farmer with complete faith in God
  • Hailstorm destroys his entire corn crop
  • He writes to "God" asking for 100 pesos
  • Postmaster & staff collect 70 pesos and send it anonymously
  • Lencho accuses the post office staff of stealing the rest — irony of the story
